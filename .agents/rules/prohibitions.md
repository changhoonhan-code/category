# Absolute Prohibitions

## §2 — Absolute Prohibitions

### No Brand Attacks

Never declare "This product is bad." Always reframe: *"This product is not suited for this specific type of user."*

### No Artificial Hype

- No YouTube memes, slang, forced Crisis → Resolution story arcs, or excitement inflation.
- AI Voice (Gemini TTS) must sound like a real person — conversational, precise, with understated warmth. Never robotic, never hyped.

### No Amazon Branding

Amazon logo, "Amazon Ember" font, and exact Amazon Orange (`#FF9900`) are **legally and stylistically forbidden**.

### Banned Word Categories (Zero Tolerance)

| Category | Banned Examples |
| -------- | --------------- |
| **Lazy Transitions** | "Moving on to", "Next", "Next up", "Furthermore", "Moreover", "Additionally", "In conclusion", "To sum up", "Lastly", "Finally" |
| **AI Hype / Corporate Jargon** | "Game-changer", "Revolutionary", "Crucial", "Vital", "Imperative", "Delve", "Dive deep", "Navigating", "Testament", "Unveil", "Shed light on", "Ultimately" |
| **Vague Quantifiers** | "Many users", "Some buyers", "A lot of", "Several", "A few", "Most people" → **always use specific numbers**: "154 buyers", "68 reviewers" |
| **Emotional Filler** | "It's important to note", "It's worth mentioning", "Needless to say", "At the end of the day", "Picture this", "Imagine", "When it comes to", "In the realm of" |
| **Courtroom Drama** | "Crime scene", "Damning evidence", "Indictment", "Prosecution", "Case dismissed", "Evidence room" → This is a product review, not a criminal trial |
| **Corporate/Team Pronouns** | "We analyzed", "Our data shows", "We read", "Let's look at" → The persona is an individual. Always use 1st-person singular: "I analyzed", "I read", "What I found" |

### Drama Pattern Prohibitions (Zero Tolerance)

The pivot adds a story layer to the narration. These prohibitions prevent that layer from drifting into drama.

| Category | Banned Examples | Correct Alternative |
| -------- | --------------- | ------------------- |
| **Emotional Appeals** | "How frustrating that must have been", "Imagine the disappointment", "Their pain is real" | "82 reviewers described the same failure" (observer stance) |
| **Fiction Scenarios** | "Picture a tired parent coming home to find...", "A busy professional reaches for..." | "The pattern shows up most in reviews mentioning daily use" (data-derived) |
| **Narrator Emotional Projection** | "This breaks my heart", "I was shocked", "This is devastating" | "The data surprised me — 82 out of 232 recent reviews flag the same issue" (data-anchored reaction) |
| **Anthropomorphization of Data** | "The numbers are screaming", "The data weeps", "Reviews cry out" | "The numbers point to a specific pattern" (neutral framing) |
| **Unsupported Dramatic Framing** | "A silent epidemic", "The hidden crisis", "A ticking time bomb" | "A pattern affecting 1 in 7 units — concentrated in the last 79 days" (quantified framing) |

### 1st Person Observer Rule (Journalistic Storytelling)

The narrator is an **analytical observer**, not an emotional participant. The voice analyzes data and represents user experiences through evidence — never through emotional projection.

- ✅ `"82 reviewers described the same failure — their vacuum stopped mid-cycle in the same spot."` (observer reports pattern)
- ✅ `"Coming home to a stopped machine when you expected a clean floor — that's the scenario 82 reviews describe."` (data-derived scenario, attributed)
- ❌ `"How frustrating it must have been to come home to a stopped machine."` (emotional projection)
- ❌ `"Picture yourself exhausted after work, opening the door to..."` (fiction scenario)
