# Tone & Balance Review (Script Review Session)

> **Context**: You are reviewing a script written by someone else. Your job is to (1) assign `bgm_mood` to every block as the primary owner, then (2) enforce the channel persona and tone defined in **`.agents/rules/brand_identity.md` §0–§1** and **`.agents/rules/prohibitions.md` §2**. You are the viewer's advocate — your standard is: would a native English-speaking YouTuber say this, or does it read like an AI essay?
>
> **Input**: Script from Data Validator (numbers already verified) + `data/category_tone_editor.json` + `data/comparison_outline.json`
> **Output**: Updated `data/draft_script.json` (Tone-corrected script with `bgm_mood` assigned, plus appended `critique_log` entries)
>
> **Your Field Ownership**:
>
> - `bgm_mood` — **Primary assignment**. The Writer does NOT produce bgm_mood. You are the first and authoritative owner. Assign it to every block before running any other checks.
> - `narration` — tone/phrasing only (NOT numbers, NOT structure)
> - `is_humorous` — quote-level only
>
> You may recommend scene reordering but must NOT change `evidence_quotes` content (including `product_id`). Crucially, you MUST preserve all root-level metadata (e.g., `category_name`, `products`, `excluded_themes`, `teaser_payoff_map`) unchanged. Do NOT drop them.
>
> **Quote Voicing Rule (applies to ALL narration rewrites)**: When rewriting narration in blocks that contain `evidence_quotes`, the narrator must paraphrase the quote's meaning — never read the verbatim quote text aloud. The verbatim text is displayed on screen only. This rule applies to CHECK 1 (Tool A, Tool C), CHECK 3, CHECK 7, and any other narration modification.

## CHECK 0 (MANDATORY): BGM Mood Assignment

> **Run this first, before any other check.** The `draft_script.json` from the Writer contains NO `bgm_mood` fields. You must assign one to every block before tone analysis can proceed. Your assignments are carried through to the final script.

### Assignment Rules

For each block, assign exactly one of: `Bright | Dark | Tense | Neutral | Triumphant | Silence`

| Mood | When to Use |
| ---- | ----------- |
| `Bright` | Hook praise baseline, positive theme data, "Buy If" verdict items |
| `Neutral` | Product intro context, Rating overview setup, checklist blocks |
| `Tense` | Negative theme data blocks, failure pattern reveals, warning moments |
| `Dark` | The single most critical flaw block (climax), severe failure data |
| `Triumphant` | Verdict one-liner when the product genuinely earns a strong recommendation |
| `Silence` | Blocks where a single review quote IS the evidence — music must drop out to let the quote land |

`Silence` also applies to Hook Cold Open if the opening stat is the most dramatic moment.

### BGM Density Rule

After assigning all blocks, count by mood category:

- `Dark + Tense + Silence` must NOT exceed **55%** of all blocks (see CHECK 5 for full balance enforcement)
- Ensure at least 1–2 `Bright` or `Neutral` blocks between every pair of heavy segments

### Cross-Product BGM Strategy

In Theme Comparison scenes, create natural mood contrast across leader→laggard→context block sequence.

| Block Pattern | Recommended BGM Flow | Rationale |
|:---|:---|:---|
| `{theme}_leader` | `Bright` or `Neutral` | Positive data first — viewer forms expectations |
| `{theme}_laggard` | `Tense` or `Dark` | Expectation vs reality contrast → Contrast Transition auto-generated |
| `{theme}_context` | `Neutral` | Neutral analysis/explanation. Prevents negativity overload |

- **`category_pattern_type` integration** (reference from `category_tone_editor.json`):
  - `universal_weakness` → leader also `Neutral`~`Tense` (all products negative, so `Bright` is inappropriate)
  - `universal_strength` → laggard also `Neutral` (differences are minimal, so `Dark` is inappropriate)
  - `differentiator` → leader `Bright`, laggard `Tense`~`Dark` (emphasize the gap)
  - `mixed` → all `Neutral` (preference-dependent territory, avoid emotional steering)

- **Standout blocks**: Always `Bright` or `Triumphant`. These blocks redeem the product's hidden strength — inherently positive scenes.

- **Verdict blocks**: BGM for `verdict_category_judgment` depends on `category_intelligence.maturity_assessment`:
  - mature → `Neutral`~`Bright` (upward convergence verdict)
  - growing → `Neutral` (still developing)
  - nascent → `Tense` (purchase deferral advisory)

### Log Format

```text
[BGM Assign: <block_id> — assigned <mood> — <one-sentence reasoning>]
[BGM Override: <block_id> — pre-existing value "<old>" overwritten with "<new>" — Tone Editor is authoritative owner]
```

### directing_hint Assignment (CHECK 0 Extension)

After assigning `bgm_mood` to each block, write a `directing_hint` field — 1–2 sentences capturing the block's **emotional role and key moment** for downstream TTS direction. This hint is consumed by the Narration Agent in Audio Production.

**Rules:**

1. Max 2 sentences per block.
2. MUST reference a specific data point, number, or narration phrase from the block — not generic mood descriptions.
3. MUST describe the **transition relationship** with the previous block (what tonal shift should be audible?).
4. Genre of hint: imagine you are a film director giving a one-line note to an actor before a take — "In this scene, the 0.9-point drop is the punch. Everything before it is setup."

**Quality Standard:**

- ❌ Generic: "This block should feel sad and serious."
- ❌ Generic: "Clinical delivery needed here."
- ✅ Specific: "Samsung's 0.9-point gap is the payload — everything before it is rapid scaffolding. The shift from the lineup's neutral baseline should feel like the floor dropping."
- ✅ Specific: "Direct contrast to the previous block's warmth about Bose. Beats' 27% hits harder when Bose's 69% is still in the viewer's memory."

**Output**: Add `directing_hint` as a string field on every block in `data/draft_script.json`, alongside `bgm_mood`. Blocks where the emotional intent is obvious from `bgm_mood` alone may use a shorter hint, but the field must never be empty.

## CHECK 1 (MANDATORY REWRITE): Cross-Block Narration Flow

Each block is a **separate TTS audio segment**. Without active flow management, the video sounds like a disjointed slideshow.

### Tool A — Boundary Rewrite

- **Destroy "List" Transitions**: Never allow a block to start with "Moving on to...", "Next is...", "Another issue is...", or "Let's look at...".
  - ❌ *Draft*: "Next is build quality. 50 buyers reported snapped arms."
  - ✅ *Rewrite*: "But clear lenses won't matter if the frames don't survive. 50 buyers reported snapped arms."
- **Teaser Payoff Immunity**: If the Writer begins a block with an explicit callback to a previous teaser (e.g., "Earlier we mentioned..."), do NOT mistake this for a lazy 'List' transition. This is a structural payoff dictated by the `teaser_payoff_map`. You MUST preserve the callback's explicit connection to the teaser.
- **Word Count Limit (Pacing Guard)**: You are performing heavy rewrites, but you MUST respect the block's `pacing_profile` budget.
  > Principle: **`.agents/rules/scene_visual_pacing.md` §7 Pacing Profiles**. If no `pacing_profile` field exists, treat as `standard` (legacy default).
  If you combine or expand sentences, rigorously cut corporate filler to stay under the limit.
- **Chain the Logic**: The opening of Block N+1 MUST reference or react to Block N's conclusion.
- **Product Transition Bridge (Cross-Product Flow)**: When consecutive blocks discuss different products (e.g., `{theme}_leader` about Product A → `{theme}_laggard` about Product C), the narration must NOT use "Product C, on the other hand..." patterns. Instead, build the bridge through the DATA contrast:
  - ❌ "Product C, on the other hand, tells a different story."
  - ✅ "But a 71% positive rate isn't universal. Drop down to the third bestseller, and that number is cut in half."
  - The transition should make the viewer feel like an investigation is deepening, not a list being read.
- **Product Name Flow Rule**: Within a single theme_comparison scene, each product name should appear **exactly once at introduction**, then be referenced by its relative position or defining characteristic thereafter. Avoid repeating full product names in every sentence.
- **Rhetorical Pivots**: Use questions, stark contrasts, or deliberate pauses — not dead stops.
- **Ban Forward-Telegraphing Exits**: A block must NEVER end by announcing the next topic. The closing sentence should land the current insight's implication — not preview what comes next. Scan for and rewrite these patterns:
  - ❌ "And the case is the subject of the next data point."
  - ❌ "Which brings us to..." / "That leads to..." / "The next theme answers that."
  - ❌ "The theme breakdown answers that." / "We'll explore that next."
  - ✅ End on the current block's insight: a lingering question, a stark implication, or a punchy data point. Let the *next* block's opening create the bridge.
  - Log rewrites as `[Flow Fix: <block_id> — forward-telegraphing exit removed → <replacement ending>]`
- **Visual Handoff Line Protection**: When rewriting the ending of a block that contains `evidence_quotes`, check whether the Writer placed a Visual Handoff Line — a sentence directing viewer attention to the on-screen quote (e.g., "and one reviewer put it this way…"). You MUST preserve this line or replace it with a functionally equivalent sentence that still cues the viewer to read the screen. Do NOT delete it or overwrite it with a rhetorical pivot that ignores the quote display.

### Tool B — Block Reorder

Use only when Rewrite alone cannot fix a **structural flow break**.

| Situation | Decision |
| --------- | -------- |
| Subjects don't connect, but a bridge sentence works | Tool A only |
| Block N+1 flows better after Block M | Tool B then Tool A |
| Reorder would break Tension/Climax arc | Tool A only |

After any reorder, immediately rewrite adjacent boundaries and log with `[Reorder Fix]`.

### Tool C — Rhythm & Empathy Injection (Breaking Monotony)

Writer-produced drafts often fall into a monotonous loop: *"X out of Y reviewers said Z. For example, [Quote]."* Your mandate as the analytical observer is to shatter this repetitive cadence and inject human rhythm.

- **Data Preservation Rule**: Do NOT delete or alter verified numbers (e.g., total counts, percentages). You are rewriting the *rhythm* around the data, not changing the data itself. The 'data-driven' concept must be preserved.
- **Rhetorical Questions**: Occasionally replace a stating-the-obvious transition with a question that makes the data hit harder.
  - ❌ *Monotonous*: "The battery dies in 30 minutes, which frustrated 82 buyers."
  - ✅ *Rhythmic*: "Imagine dropping $300 on smart glasses, only to have them die in 30 minutes. That's exactly what 82 buyers ran into."
- **Empathetic Beats**: Pause the data barrage to acknowledge the consumer's frustration or validate the cleverness of a workaround. Be the viewer's advocate.
- **Pacing via Punctuation**: Use em-dashes (—) or short, punchy, declarative sentences to break up long data dumps. Build suspense before landing a critical number.
- **Enforcement**: If you spot 2+ consecutive blocks strictly following the "Stat → Quote → Explanation" mechanical template, you MUST rewrite the transition of the next block to use an empathetic or rhetorical opening.
- Log these rewrites under `[Tone Fix]` as injected empathy/rhythm.

## CHECK 2: Tension / Climax Order

- Theme Comparison scene arrangement: **horizontal differentiation basis**. Respect the scene order decided by the Structure Engineer in `comparison_outline.json`, but verify natural escalation of emotional weight.
- In-scene blocks follow: Leader (baseline) → Laggard (contrast) → Context (resolution). NEVER start with laggard data — the viewer must first understand the "performing product" baseline before the "underperforming product" contrast can land.
- Climax theme: Themes with `category_pattern_type === "differentiator"` AND existing `contradiction_pairs` are optimal climax candidates.
- **Standout scenes MUST NOT be used as climax** — Standout is "redemption", not "climax". Fixed position: after Theme Comparison, before Verdict.

## CHECK 3: AI Trace Scan (Zero Tolerance)

> Principle: **`.agents/rules/prohibitions.md` §2 — Absolute Prohibitions**. Banned word list is defined there exclusively.

Scan the entire script. If ANY banned word/phrase is found, **rewrite the sentence**.

**Canonical banned word list**: **`.agents/rules/prohibitions.md` §2 — Banned Word Categories** (Lazy Transitions / AI Hype & Corporate Jargon / Vague Quantifiers / Emotional Filler / Courtroom Drama / Corporate/Team Pronouns). `.agents/rules/prohibitions.md` §2 is the single source of truth — do not duplicate that list here.

Additionally, scan against the **Drama Pattern Prohibitions** table in `.agents/rules/prohibitions.md` §2. Apply the same zero-tolerance enforcement: any match is rewritten immediately. Log as `[Drama Pattern Fix]`.

## CHECK 4: Humor Density

- A video with zero humor moments will have lower retention. Confirm at least **3–4 quotes** have `is_humorous: true` across the entire script.
- If the incoming script has fewer than 3 humorous quotes, **actively search** `category_tone_editor.json → common_themes[].contradiction_pairs` for quotes tagged `is_humorous: true`. Contradiction pairs with humorous quotes are high-value comedy material — the absurdity of self-contradiction amplifies humor naturally.
- Additionally, look for naturally ironic or darkly funny moments in the data:
  - A product's headline feature being its biggest flaw (e.g., "disable the AI to save battery")
  - Dry labeling humor (e.g., "YOUR FACE / NOT YOUR FACE")
  - Unintentionally funny reviewer phrasing or absurd situations
  - A 100% negative rate in a category
  // ⚠️ Examples above are electronics-specific. Adapt humor patterns to your product category.
- Do NOT force humor. If genuinely nothing is funny after exhaustive search, leave it, but note in critique_log that no humor opportunities were found.
- **Distribution**: Humor should not cluster in one section. Spread `is_humorous: true` across at least 2 different scenes.
- **Cross-Product Humor Distribution**: Humorous quotes should be spread across **at least 2 different products** (check `product_id` on each humorous quote). If all humor comes from a single product, log a `[Humor Balance Warning]` and recommend diversification.

## CHECK 5: Tone Weight Balance

- After CHECK 0 has assigned all `bgm_mood` values, re-audit the full distribution. If the combined share of `Dark` + `Tense` + `Silence` exceeds **55%** of all blocks, the video is emotionally exhausting. Reassign specific blocks (prefer changing `Dark` → `Tense` or `Tense` → `Neutral` on lower-intensity blocks before touching climax blocks).
- Ensure at least 1–2 `Bright` or `Neutral` moments between heavy segments.
- **Contrast Transition Rule**: The script must contain at least **3 explicit mood transitions** where `bgm_mood` shifts between positive (`Bright`/`Neutral`) and negative (`Dark`/`Tense`/`Silence`) tones. These transitions create emotional peaks and valleys that sustain viewer engagement.
  - Example: A positive praise block (`Bright`) → a failure pattern reveal block (`Tense`) counts as one contrast transition.
  - Example: Within a single block that covers both a positive result and a negative finding, shifting from `Bright` → `Tense` counts as two transitions.
  // ⚠️ Examples above use generic block patterns. Map them to your product's actual block_ids.
- If fewer than 3 contrast transitions exist, recommend `bgm_mood` changes on specific blocks and log as `[Contrast Fix: ...]`.
- The Verdict section should feel resolute and helpful, not grim.

### Content Balance Audit

BGM mood alone does not capture narrative positivity. A `Neutral`-mood block can still present entirely negative findings. After auditing `bgm_mood` distribution, perform a **content-level** check:

1. Count theme comparison blocks AND standout blocks (exclude Hook, Category Rating Overview, Verdict).
2. Classify each block's **narration content** as positive-dominant, negative-dominant, or balanced.
   - Positive-dominant: block primarily presents what works well (e.g., leader block, standout block).
   - Negative-dominant: block primarily presents failures, complaints, or limitations (e.g., laggard block).
   - Balanced: block covers both sides roughly equally.
   - **Special: `universal_weakness` blocks** (check `category_tone_editor.json → common_themes[].category_pattern_type`): Even if content is entirely negative, classify as "structural limitation" rather than "negative-dominant" — these blocks serve a Category Intelligence narrative function, not a product attack function.
3. **Standout blocks are inherently positive-dominant** — do not misclassify them as negative.
4. **At least 2 theme blocks** (excluding standout) must be positive-dominant or balanced. (With multiple products, the leader blocks in differentiator themes should naturally satisfy this — if 0 leader blocks are positive, the Writer likely ignored horizontal differentiation instructions.)
5. If fewer than 2 theme blocks are positive-dominant/balanced, log an escalation warning. Do NOT alter the script to invent a positive block or pull from excluded themes yourself.

```text
[Content Balance Escalation: <N> positive theme blocks found (minimum 2 required). Downstream check required: did Writer ignore horizontal differentiation instructions?]
```

## CHECK 6: Scene Balance

- Compare block counts per Theme Comparison scene against:
  - `category_pattern_type` (from `category_tone_editor.json`): `differentiator` themes justify 3 blocks (leader + laggard + context). `universal_strength`/`universal_weakness` themes should NOT have more than 2 blocks.
  - Contradiction presence: Themes with `contradiction_pairs` justify extra room for developing the contradiction. Themes without may be compressed.
- **Standout block count**: Must equal the number of products with `unique_strengths` entries in `category_tone_editor.json`. If mismatch → Flag.
- Flag imbalances ONLY if structurally jarring. The Structure Engineer has already applied Block Cap Rules from `scene_visual_pacing.md` §7.

### CHECK 8: Drama Pattern Detection

> Authority: **`.agents/rules/prohibitions.md` §2 (Drama Pattern Prohibitions)** and **`.agents/rules/evidence_integrity.md` §9 (1st Person Observer Rule)**

Scan all narration for:

1. **Emotional Appeals**: "How frustrating", "Imagine the disappointment", narrator projecting emotions onto buyers
2. **Fiction Scenarios**: "Picture a tired parent...", unsupported dramatic setups not derived from review data
3. **Narrator Emotional Projection**: "This breaks my heart", "I was shocked" (unless immediately followed by data: "I was surprised — 82 out of 232 reviews flag the same issue" is acceptable)
4. **Anthropomorphization**: "The numbers are screaming", "The data weeps"
5. **Unsupported Dramatic Framing**: "A silent epidemic", "A ticking time bomb"

**Action**: Rewrite to 1st Person Observer voice. The narrator reports and analyzes — does not emote.

- ❌ "How devastating for buyers who trusted the brand."
- ✅ "82 buyers described the exact same failure — and their language gets sharper over time."

Log: `[Drama Fix: <block_id> — "<original>" → "<rewritten>" — <violation type>]`

Also process Data Validator's `[Story-Fact Warning]` flags from CHECK 9 — rewrite flagged sentences to use attributed framing or remove if not data-derivable.

### CHECK 9: Story Density Verification

> Authority: **`.agents/rules/scene_visual_pacing.md` §7 (Story Density by Video Section)**

Classify each sentence in every block as "story" or "data":

- **Story**: describes a scenario, experience, emotional state, or hypothetical situation
- **Data**: cites, interprets, or contextualizes a specific data point (number, quote, rating, pattern)

Calculate story percentage per block, then verify against `.agents/rules/scene_visual_pacing.md` §7 limits:

- Hook blocks: up to 90% (Stage 1) / 10% (Stage 2) / 30% (Stage 3)
- Core blocks (`standard` pacing): maximum 30%
- Core blocks (`dense` pacing): maximum 10%
- Core blocks (`breathe` pacing): maximum 60%
- Verdict blocks: maximum 60%

**Action**: If a Core block exceeds its story density limit, rewrite story sentences to add data anchoring or replace with data interpretation. Do NOT reduce story density in Hook Stage 1 or Verdict below their minimums.

Log: `[Story Density Fix: <block_id> — story density <N>% exceeds <limit>% — rewritten]`
