# Category Comparison Directing Examples — Reference

Full DIRECTOR'S NOTES + TRANSCRIPT examples for key category comparison scene types.
Load this file when directing multi-product comparison blocks.

For archetype definitions and technique catalog → `directing_reference.md`.
For single-archetype delivery examples → `archetypes_examples.md`.

> **These examples are PATTERN DEMONSTRATIONS — not templates.**
> They show directing patterns and TRANSCRIPT rhythm for category comparison
> scenes. DIRECTOR'S NOTES are at pattern-character level. For the full
> word-level depth expected in actual prompts, see the **Depth Calibration
> example** in `directing_reference.md`.

> **These examples use fictional placeholder products (Product A, B, C, D) and data.**
> Product names, feature descriptions, and all numeric data are illustrative only.

## Hook: Contradiction Reveal (`hook_contradiction`)

**When**: 2-block Hook, Stage 1 + Stage 2 combined. `bgm_mood: Silence` or `Tense`.
**Key**: Zero product references in Stage 1. Pitch Drop on data gap, NOT product name.

```text
### DIRECTOR'S NOTES
- Archetype: E
- Delivery: Measured curiosity building to controlled weight.
  Low-pitched transition at the data impact point.
- Tone: Category mystery → market contradiction reveal.
  Stage 1 feels like the start of a story. Stage 2 is clinical evidence.
- Key moments: "scores dead last" is the pitch-drop moment — land the data
  gap, not the product name. After the contradiction, shift to clinical register.
  "forty-two hundred verified reviews" grounds the claim — steady, factual delivery.
- Emotional arc: Measured curiosity transitioning to clinical weight at the
  data impact point — the intrigue resolves into evidence.

#### TRANSCRIPT
You buy headphones for one reason.
[short pause]
You want them to sound good.
So what happens when last month's number-one seller — the one outselling
everything else in the category —
[short pause]
scores dead last in the one thing it advertises most?
[medium pause]
That's not a hot take. That's forty-two hundred verified reviews across four products
pointing at the same conclusion.
```

> **Directing Intent**: The `[short pause]` before "scores dead last" creates the micro-pause. The pitch-drop intent is in DIRECTOR'S NOTES Key moments. TRANSCRIPT uses pacing tags only — emotional color lives in Notes.

## Rating Overview: Lineup Scaffolding (`rating_overview_lineup`)

**When**: First block of Category Rating Overview scene. All product names must appear here (SEO: within first 60 seconds).
**Key**: Every product gets identical vocal weight. Lineup, not ranking.

```text
### DIRECTOR'S NOTES
- Archetype: F
- Delivery: Warm, slightly elevated pitch, flowing and natural.
  Brisk, clear scaffolding — every product name lands cleanly but none
  should be emphasized over another. This is a lineup, not a ranking.
- Tone: Energy resets to neutral baseline after Hook curiosity loop.
  BSR context is a factual qualifier, not emphasized.
- Key moments: Product list must have identical emphasis per item —
  counteract TTS "descending list intonation" (max energy on first item).
  "headline numbers" shifts to forward-leaning curiosity for next block.
- Emotional arc: Warm, neutral scaffolding on the opener, brisk clinical
  enumeration through the lineup, then rising curiosity seeding the next block.

#### TRANSCRIPT
Four products. All Amazon top sellers based on last month's sales volume.
[extremely fast]
Product A — four point three stars, sixty-two hundred total ratings.
Product B — four point one stars, thirty-eight hundred ratings.
Product C — four point four stars, fifty-one hundred ratings.
Product D — four point zero stars, twenty-nine hundred ratings.
[short pause]
Those are headline numbers — the all-time cumulative average.
But headline numbers include reviews from two years ago. The recent data
tells a different story.
```

> **Directing Intent**: `[extremely fast]` for the product list ensures brisk scaffolding. Equal treatment of all items prevents implicit ranking. Curiosity for the next block is conveyed through DIRECTOR'S NOTES, not inline tags.

## Theme Comparison: Leader Block (`{theme}_leader`)

**When**: Theme comparison scene, highest positive_ratio product block. `bgm_mood: Bright` or `Neutral`.
**Key**: Present strength without crowning a winner. Warm but grounded.

```text
### DIRECTOR'S NOTES
- Archetype: F
- Delivery: Warm, slightly elevated pitch, flowing and natural.
  Genuine brightness, grounded in data. The analyst presenting
  a clear strength — not celebrating a winner.
- Tone: Data-backed brightness. Vocal Smile territory.
- Context: Previous block ended with category rating overview.
- Key moments: "seventy-eight percent" gets brief Vocal Smile — contained, not celebratory.
  Chew each digit of "seventy-eight" with deliberate separation.
  "without the charging case trick" is the differentiator — slight emphasis
  on "without" to land the contrast.
- Emotional arc: Warm confidence on the opening — softer register presenting
  a clear strength. Contained brightness on the key percentage.

#### TRANSCRIPT
Product C's battery data tells a straightforward story.
[speaking slowly]
seventy-eight percent of battery-related reviews are positive — the highest ratio in the category.
Users consistently describe ten-to-twelve-hour runtimes under mixed-use conditions.
[short pause]
That's without the charging case trick that other products in this lineup need
to hit similar numbers.
```

> **Directing Intent**: The brightness of Archetype F is natural and restrained. `[speaking slowly]` on "seventy-eight percent" forces clear pronunciation. DIRECTOR'S NOTES handle Vocal Smile, emotional color, and deliberate pronunciation; TRANSCRIPT carries pacing only.

## Theme Comparison: Laggard Block (`{theme}_laggard`)

**When**: Theme comparison scene, lowest positive_ratio product block. `bgm_mood: Dark` or `Tense`.
**Key**: Register shift from Leader block IS the directing. Clinical, not mocking.

```text
### DIRECTOR'S NOTES
- Archetype: C
- Delivery: Full register shift from previous block. Clinical, low-pitched,
  unemotional. No warmth carryover — the contrast between blocks IS the
  directing. Sustained gravity throughout.
- Tone: Matter-of-fact data exposure. Not mocking the product — exposing
  a structural data pattern.
- Context: "That's without the charging case trick that other products
  in this lineup need to hit similar numbers."
- Key moments: "sixty-two percent negative" gets steady clinical weight — no rush.
  Chew each digit of "sixty-two" with deliberate separation.
  "four point two hours" gets slight tempo drop. "outselling every competitor" —
  volume taper, let the irony speak for itself.

#### TRANSCRIPT
[speaking slowly]
Product A tells a different story.
[short pause]
[speaking slowly]
sixty-two percent of battery mentions are negative — the highest negative ratio in the
category by a twenty-point margin.
[short pause]
The median reported runtime is four point two hours.
[medium pause]
That's the same product outselling every competitor last month.
```

> **Directing Intent**: The archetype shift from Leader(F) to Laggard(C) is the core of Contrast Directing. The **register shift in DIRECTOR'S NOTES** conveys the gap. TRANSCRIPT uses `[speaking slowly]` for clinical weight and standard pauses for pacing. Numbers are spelled-out with pronunciation direction in Notes.

## Theme Comparison: Universal Weakness (`{theme}_context`)

**When**: Optional third block in theme comparison. All products share the same weakness.
**Key**: Step back from individual products. Category-level structural observation.

```text
### DIRECTOR'S NOTES
- Archetype: E
- Delivery: Deliberate, thoughtful, rising intrigue. The analyst stepping
  back from individual products to observe a category-wide pattern.
  Slightly sobered — not dramatic.
- Tone: Reflective Discovery. Panoramic, systemic finding.
- Context: "Product B scores higher in initial comfort, but the same
  two-month degradation pattern appears across three hundred forty reviews."
- Key moments: "all four of them" gets steady clinical weight — equal emphasis.
  "category-level material limitation" is the thesis — slight emphasis on
  "category" with a pause before delivery.
- Emotional arc: Deliberate, reflective stepping-back from individual products.
  Measured sobriety building toward the structural thesis.

#### TRANSCRIPT
Here's where individual product scores stop mattering.
[short pause]
Every product in this lineup — all four of them — shows the same pattern.
Comfort scores are high at purchase. They degrade between month two and month four.
The degradation rate varies. The trajectory does not.
[short pause]
This isn't a Product A problem or a Product C problem.
[medium pause]
[speaking slowly]
This is a category-level material limitation.
```

> **Directing Intent**: Universal Weakness highlights a **category-wide structural limitation**, not individual product defects. Reflective register is conveyed through DIRECTOR'S NOTES emotional arc, not inline tags. The thesis sentence gets `[speaking slowly]` after a `[medium pause]`.

## Standout: Orthogonal Value Spotlight (`standout_{product_id}`)

**When**: After all theme comparisons, before verdict. Product's unique killer strength.
**Key**: Not rescuing a loser — presenting data the comparison table couldn't capture.

```text
### DIRECTOR'S NOTES
- Archetype: F
- Delivery: Warm, slightly elevated pitch, flowing and natural.
  Controlled enthusiasm. Genuine interest without advocacy. The analyst
  is not rescuing the product — they are presenting data that the
  comparison tables couldn't capture.
- Tone: Factual reset → data-backed brightness. Not a sales pitch.
- Context: Previous block was the last theme_comparison scene.
- Key moments: Weakness acknowledgment first ("lost the battery comparison")
  is matter-of-fact. "three hundred forty reviewers" pivots to genuine brightness — brief
  Vocal Smile. "not a consolation prize" is the analytical close — even,
  clinical, no emotional plea.
- Emotional arc: Matter-of-fact acknowledgment of weakness, then pivot to
  warm, data-backed brightness on the unique value. Close with clinical certainty.

#### TRANSCRIPT
Now — Product B lost the battery comparison. It placed last in noise isolation.
[short pause]
But three hundred forty reviewers specifically mention one thing no other product in this lineup
offers: full EQ customization through its companion app.
If you're the kind of user who dials in a specific sound profile for podcasts during
commutes and switches to a bass-heavy preset for workouts —
[short pause]
this is the only product in the top four that lets you do that without third-party
software.
[medium pause]
That's not a consolation prize. For that user profile, it's the reason to buy.
```

> **Directing Intent**: Acknowledge weakness before pivoting to Orthogonal Value. Factual reset → warm brightness → clinical close — all emotional shifts conveyed through DIRECTOR'S NOTES, not inline tags.

## Verdict: Use-Case Picks (`verdict_use_case_picks`)

**When**: Verdict scene, horizontal use-case recommendations. `bgm_mood: Triumphant` or `Neutral`.
**Key**: Every recommendation carries identical vocal weight. No product is "the winner."

```text
### DIRECTOR'S NOTES
- Archetype: G
- Delivery: Firm, even, confident. Verdict-like register. Trusted advisor
  delivering clear, use-case-based recommendations. NEVER frame any product
  as "the winner" or "the best." Each recommendation carries identical
  vocal weight — no product gets more enthusiasm than another.
- Tone: Authoritative map-laying. Horizontal, not hierarchical.
- Key moments: Each "If...then Product X" pair must have identical delivery
  weight. Counteract TTS descending list intonation — last item gets same
  energy as first. Trap candidate warning is clinical observation, not accusation.
- Emotional arc: Deliberate, reflective authority throughout — the trusted
  advisor laying out clear paths. Even warmth, identical weight per recommendation.

#### TRANSCRIPT
If battery life is your non-negotiable — you commute two hours a day and
need all-day runtime without a case —
[short pause]
Product C is where the data points.
If sound customization matters more — you want granular EQ control across
different listening scenarios —
[short pause]
Product B is the only option in this lineup.
If you want the smallest form factor and don't mind charging twice a day —
[short pause]
Product D fits that profile.
[medium pause]
And if you're about to buy last month's top seller because it's the
most popular —
[short pause]
you should know that Product A's data tells a very specific story.
One we've spent the last twelve minutes documenting.
```

> **Directing Intent**: Enforce **identical energy** across all recommendations. `[short pause]` before each recommendation creates consistent rhythm. The trap warning uses `[medium pause]` for distinct cadence — "clinical, not accusatory" per DIRECTOR'S NOTES.
